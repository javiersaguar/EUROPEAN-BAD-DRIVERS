# Limitations and extension gates

- **Coverage:** DGT includes injury crashes, not the universe of property-damage collisions or insurance claims. The analyzed period is 2022–2024; earlier years need a separate schema and comparability audit.
- **Exposure:** population, vehicle and permit stocks are proxies. Visitors, commuters, trips, kilometres, traffic density and fleet composition are unobserved. Crashes at a location cannot be attributed to its residents' ability. Stocks refer to residence/registration and different reference dates. Vehicles exclude mopeds and include trailers; permit holders exclude special licences.
- **Missingness:** vehicle/permit exposure is observed in every province-year in 2022–2024. Unknown municipality and collision codes prevent complete finer-group comparisons. Age, sex, licence tenure and individual vehicle age/type are absent in the crash workbook, so demographic/vehicle risk estimates are unavailable.
- **Intervals:** Poisson limits and the event bootstrap describe specified stochastic models. They do not correct underreporting, selection bias, correlated fatalities, exposure error or spatial dependence. Small samples and wide intervals should constrain interpretation.
- **Composite:** overlapping components and subjective weights can amplify some outcomes. Normalization, cohort and metric choices affect the ranking. Within-year score changes are not absolute safety improvements. Default weights are an experiment, not a validated definition of driver quality.
- **Europe:** 30-day fatalities are relatively comparable, but source flags, follow-up and registration differ. Population is not travel exposure. No European injury/claim composite is published. Country comparability metadata retains actual status flags; it is not a claim that every country has identical data quality.
- **Insurance:** the audited Insurance Europe workbook ends in 2016, has heterogeneous coverage/units and permits alternative exposure denominators in its notes. The UNESPA 2024 PDF now supplies a separate material-damage explorer: national coverage shares/costs, selected municipal relative differences and all-coverage provincial volumes. Its 40 cities per coverage are published extremes, not a complete municipal panel. Municipal claims/exposures and absolute probabilities are unavailable; material claims can coexist with bodily injury. National shares are rounded and provincial totals do not fully reconcile. See [insurance](insurance.md). A modern European claims comparison still requires matched coverage counts, insured vehicle-years, country notes and a common time panel.
- **Vehicle-kilometres:** the audited transport table covers specific road ownership/networks. The 2022 RCE panel uses DGT state-owned-road injury crashes, province/year/all-vehicle matching, and retains inventory caveats. It has 44 exposed provinces and 35 state-road crashes in eight provinces without matching VKT; missing rates stay missing. Using it under an all-road numerator would create a coverage mismatch.
- **ML:** reported severity is conditional on inclusion in an injury-crash registry. Predictive associations are not causal, and the model is not an individual annual accident forecast. Post-event attributes limit prospective deployment. The fixed 0.5 threshold has very low severe-case recall and is not a screening policy. A separate F2 study selects the threshold with 2023 scores from a 2022-only fitted model; its 2024 report is exploratory because that year was already inspected. A future untouched year is required. Explainability depends on model coding and correlated predictors. Calibration can drift outside 2024; sparse bins are unstable.

Municipality models require reliable geographic coverage, matched exposure and a small-area stability method. Personal accident-risk modeling requires an exposed cohort with non-crash observations, appropriate driver/vehicle variables and out-of-time evaluation. Neither gate is satisfied here. No observations, exposures or probabilities are manufactured to bypass these limits.
